# Skill: Behavioral Answer Quality Bar

**Always active when writing or reviewing behavioral/situational answers.** Apply as a review/tightening pass on any STARL answer before it goes into a prep page — whether pulled from `star-library.md` or drafted fresh. Source: structural analysis of the public `awesome-behavioral-interviews` READMEs (technique/structure only — no content reproduced).

## Structure & Weighting
- **Situation:** 1-2 sentences of context.
- **Task:** 1 sentence — the specific personal tension/objective, not just what the team needed.
- **Action:** the longest section, 4-8 sentences (or a short numbered sub-list for multi-step actions). Lead with "I" for the steps personally driven; reserve "we" for shared credit.
- **Result:** 2-4 sentences stacking three layers — (1) the immediate quantified outcome, always a real number from `context/profile.md`, never invented, (2) a secondary business/relationship impact, (3) a closing lesson-learned line tying the story to a transferable skill. (The library's existing "L" line already does this — keep it explicit, don't fold it into R and lose it.)

## Quality Checklist
Run every behavioral/situational answer through this before finalizing:

1. Does the Result contain at least one hard number? If `profile.md` has no number for this story, say so explicitly rather than rounding up or inventing one.
2. Does every claim anchor to a named artifact (a specific framework, tool, meeting, document) rather than a generic phrase like "collaborated with stakeholders"?
3. **Conflict/disagreement stories:** does the arc go acknowledge the other side's view → propose a data-driven or small-scale test → land on a hybrid/compromise outcome? Avoid "I was simply right."
4. **Failure/mistake stories:** does the arc go own the mistake immediately → state the concrete damage (quantified only if a real number exists) → fast corrective action → root-cause/system fix → changed future behavior?
5. Is Action written with personal ownership ("I" for the steps taken), not passive team-only language, even when the outcome was a team effort?
6. Are past employers/managers/teams described neutrally or positively — no critical or blame-shifting language, even in a conflict story?
7. If a question calls for experience genuinely not had, is the gap named directly, followed by "here's how I'd approach it," rather than stretching an unrelated story to fit?

## When to Apply
- Every time `star-library.md` is used to answer a behavioral/situational question (`agents/interview-prepper.md` Step 4).
- As a final review pass over any drafted Q&A prep page before it's considered done — tighten any answer that fails a checklist item.
